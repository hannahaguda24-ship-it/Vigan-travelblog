About Vigan

The Best-Preserved Spanish Colonial Town in Asia

Vigan City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in the province of Ilocos Sur, Philippines. Established in 1572 by Spanish conquistador Juan de Salcedo, this historic city stands as a remarkable testament to the Philippines' colonial past.

What makes Vigan unique is its exceptional fusion of cultural influences—Spanish colonial architecture, Chinese trading heritage, and Filipino craftsmanship—all perfectly preserved in its cobblestone streets, ancestral houses, and centuries-old traditions.

Today, Vigan continues to thrive as a living museum where locals still reside in heritage homes, practice traditional crafts like abel weaving and burnay pottery, and celebrate their rich Ilocano culture through vibrant festivals and time-honored customs.

Living History

1572 Founded

Spanish Colonial Era

Founded in 1572 by Juan de Salcedo, Vigan became a vital trading post between Spanish galleons and Chinese junks. The town's unique Spanish-Filipino-Chinese urban planning created the iconic grid pattern and bahay na bato architecture you see today.

UNESCO World Heritage

Inscribed in 1999 as the best-preserved example of a Spanish colonial town in Asia. Vigan survived WWII bombing that destroyed other Philippine cities, preserving 233 historic structures.

Language

Ilocano is spoken here—known as the "language of the frugal." Try saying "Naimbag nga bigat" (Good morning) to locals!

Traditions

Abel weaving and burnay pottery continue unchanged for centuries. Watch artisans work exactly as their ancestors did.

Must-See Attractions

Calle Crisologo

The most famous street in Vigan, lined with well-preserved ancestral houses, antique shops, and cobblestone pathways. Best experienced at night when vintage lamps illuminate the historic facades.

Vigan Cathedral

St. Paul's Metropolitan Cathedral, built in 1758, showcases impressive Baroque architecture with its distinctive yellow facade and separate bell tower.

Pagburnayan

Traditional pottery workshop where artisans create burnay jars using techniques passed down through generations. Visitors can try making their own pottery.

Plaza Salcedo

The main plaza featuring a monument to founder Juan de Salcedo and a spectacular dancing fountain show every evening at 7:30 PM.

Kalesa Ride

Experience a horse-drawn carriage ride through heritage streets. This traditional mode of transport costs around ₱150 per hour.

Rock Houses

Stone houses built by Chinese traders with thick walls that naturally keep interiors cool. A unique blend of Filipino and Chinese architecture.
